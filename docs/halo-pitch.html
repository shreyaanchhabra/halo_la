HALO

An AI care team for people living with dementia and the families who look after them.

The one-liner

HALO remembers for people with dementia, keeps their medicines and routine on track, and keeps their family and doctor informed, so they can stay independent at home for longer.

What changed from the first draft

  • The G2 glasses have microphones but no camera and no speaker. HALO recognizes people by voice, not by face, and talks to the patient in short text on the display. Spoken replies are optional, through the phone or earbuds.
  • Payment blocking moved to "later". A phone or bank does it better than glasses. Spending limits stay, but only for HALO's own medicine refill orders.
  • Scam call detection moved to "later". It isn't in the core feature set, and it depends on live call audio we haven't confirmed we can get.
  • Family messages are filtered. Only contacts the family approves show up on the glasses.
  • Added a split of what runs locally and what runs in the cloud, the two portals, and the user flows.
The problem

Dementia changes every day. Help arrives in fragments.

55Mpeople live with dementia worldwide, and about 10 million more are diagnosed each year.WHO
11M+unpaid family caregivers in the US provide billions of hours of dementia care a year. Burnout is a leading reason people move into care homes.Alzheimer's Association
$3.4Bin fraud losses reported by Americans over 60 in 2023.FBI IC3

Doctors see patients a few times a year. Memory, speech and eating habits change every day, and families struggle to describe what changed between visits.

Today's tools each cover one piece: a pill reminder app, a GPS tracker, a shared calendar. None of them connect remembering, routine, family coordination and medical insight, and none of them work hands-free in the moment the patient needs them.

The solution

A team of AI agents that listens, remembers and quietly prompts

The patient wears Even Realities G2 glasses. HALO listens through the glasses' microphones, works out who is speaking, and remembers what was said and agreed. When it helps, it shows a few words on the display: who just walked in, what's planned today, that it's time for the 2 pm tablet.

The family and the doctor share a web portal. Families set HALO up, approve contacts and refills, and get a plain-language summary of each day. Doctors upload prescriptions and read a weekly trend report. HALO never diagnoses and never prescribes.

What makes HALO different
HALO never invents a memory.

An AI that confidently tells someone with dementia "your daughter called this morning" when she didn't causes real confusion and harm. A dedicated Fact-checker agent reviews everything before it reaches the display. Every statement has to point to a stored, timestamped memory: a transcript line, a calendar entry, a prescription or a logged dose. If there's no source, HALO doesn't say it. It answers honestly instead: "I'm not sure. Want to ask Maya?"

Local and cloud

What runs locally, and what runs in the cloud

The rule: anything that identifies a person (their voiceprint) or measures their cognition stays on hardware the family controls. Heavy language work uses cloud models, and only gets text with names already resolved. At the hackathon, "local" means a laptop on the same network as the glasses. In a real home it would be a small hub, such as a Raspberry Pi 5.

Two portals

Who sees what

HALO has two front ends. The patient's side is deliberately tiny: the glasses plus a large-text phone screen. All setup, review and approvals happen in the care portal, which has two roles: family and doctor.

Portal 1

Patient

Even Hub app on the G2 glasses, plus a large-text phone screen for when the glasses are off. No settings and no menus to get lost in.

Shows

  • Today: date, time, plans and who is visiting
  • Name and context when someone familiar starts talking
  • Medicine, meal and routine reminders
  • Messages from approved contacts only
  • Answers to "Ask HALO" questions, each with its source

Patient can

  • Long-press the ring or temple to ask a question
  • Tap to confirm "I took it" or "Yes, add it to my calendar"
  • Say "I had soup for lunch" to log a meal
  • Pause listening with a double-tap
Portal 2

Care portal: Family and Doctor

One web app with role-based access. Family members manage daily life; doctors see clinical data and nothing personal beyond it.

Family

  • Setup: patient profile, routine, people and voice enrollment
  • Approved contacts for messages on the glasses
  • Daily summary and "How was Dad today?" chat
  • Calendar, including plans HALO picked up from conversations
  • Refill approvals and the spending limit
  • Activity log: everything HALO showed and why
  • Forget a person or delete a memory

Doctor

  • Upload prescriptions and the care plan, then confirm the fields HALO read
  • Weekly trend report: speech, repeated questions, medicine taken, diet
  • Notes for the family that reminders can quote

User flows

How each feature plays out, step by step

GlassesPhoneCare portalHALO backend

A. Setup

Family, once, with the patient present to consent
  1. Family member creates the patient profile and invites the doctorCare portal
  2. Patient agrees to use HALO, in person with the family, and the consent is recordedCare portal
  3. Each family member and friend reads a 20-second passage to enroll their voice, and adds a short "about me"Phone
  4. Family picks approved contacts and sets the refill spending limitCare portal
  5. Doctor uploads the prescription; HALO reads it and the doctor confirms each medicine, dose and timeCare portal
  6. Patient scans the QR code once; HALO shows "Hello, Joe" on the glassesGlasses

B. Recognizing who is speaking

Voice recognition and labels
  1. Glasses stream microphone audio to the local hubGlasses
  2. A new voice starts; HALO compares it with enrolled voiceprintsBackend
  3. Confident match: show name, relationship and one recent factGlasses
  4. No match: label them "Guest 1" in the transcript and show nothingBackend
  5. Family can name a guest later, and that voice is recognized from then onCare portal

C. Plans from conversation to calendar

  1. Maya says, "I'll come by Saturday at 2 and we'll go to the park"Glasses
  2. Scheduler extracts the date, time, who and what, linked to that transcript lineBackend
  3. Glasses ask: "Add Maya, Sat 2 PM, park? Tap yes"Glasses
  4. Tap adds the event. With no tap, it waits in the portal for the family to approveCare portal
  5. On the day, the morning screen and a reminder an hour before show the visitGlasses

D. Medicine, meals and routine

  1. At a dose time from the prescription: "2 PM: Donepezil, 1 tablet"Glasses
  2. Patient taps or says "took it"; HALO logs the dose and lowers the pill countGlasses
  3. No confirmation after 30 minutes: one gentle repeat, then a note to the familyCare portal
  4. Patient mentions a meal ("had toast and tea"); HALO logs itBackend
  5. Meal reminders quote the doctor's plan only, for example "Dr. Patel: low salt". HALO makes no diet suggestions of its ownGlasses

E. Refill before it runs out

  1. Pill count drops to 7 days leftBackend
  2. Errands agent prepares a refill on the pharmacy website with Browser Use and stops before payingBackend
  3. Family gets "Refill Donepezil, $12.40, approve?"Care portal
  4. On approval within the spending limit, the order is placed and the pickup or delivery date goes on the calendarCare portal

F. Messages from approved contacts

  1. A text arrives on the patient's phonePhone
  2. Sender isn't approved: nothing shows on the glasses. It's listed in the portal for the familyCare portal
  3. Sender is approved: "Maya (granddaughter): Running 10 min late!"Glasses
  4. Patient long-presses to reply by voice; HALO reads the reply back before sendingGlasses

G. Daily summary and weekly report

  1. Throughout the day HALO logs speech metrics, doses and mealsBackend
  2. 8 PM: the family gets a plain-language summary of the dayCare portal
  3. Family can ask "How was Dad today?" and get an answer with sourcesCare portal
  4. Sunday: the doctor's weekly report charts changes against the previous weeks, with no diagnosis or adviceCare portal
Demo

"For the next 3 minutes, you are Joe"

The judge wears the G2 glasses and plays Joe, 78, who lives alone with early-stage dementia. A teammate plays Maya. The patient history is synthetic; everything in the three minutes runs live.

  1. Morning. Joe long-presses and asks what's happening today. The glasses show the day, that Maya visits at 2, and that he took his pills at 8:12.
  2. A visitor. Maya starts talking. HALO recognizes her voice and shows who she is and what's new with her.
  3. A plan. Maya says she'll come back Saturday at 2. The glasses ask to add it; Joe taps yes, and it appears on the family's calendar.
  4. Honesty. Joe asks about a call that never happened. The care portal shows the Fact-checker blocking a made-up answer, live.
  5. Medicine. A 2 PM reminder appears. Joe confirms. The pill count drops below the threshold and a refill request lands on the caregiver's phone.
  6. The family and doctor. The caregiver sees the day's summary; the doctor's view shows the week's trends.

Trust, safety and ethics

Ground rules

  • Consent. The patient sets HALO up with their family while they're able to agree. Visitors choose whether to enroll their voice, and anyone can be forgotten on request.
  • No invented memories. Every statement on the display has a stored source.
  • Transparency. The family can see everything HALO showed the patient and every action it took.
  • No diagnosis, no prescribing. HALO reports trends and repeats the doctor's instructions. It doesn't diagnose, prescribe or suggest treatment.
  • Privacy. Voiceprints and speech metrics stay local. Data is encrypted. Listening can be paused at any time, and the display shows when HALO is listening.
  • Human control. Every refill order and every alert goes to a person first.
Impact and what comes next

Who it's for, and who pays

  • Who pays: families on a monthly subscription; later, home-care agencies, care organizations and insurers who benefit when people stay home safely for longer.
  • Next: pilot with family caregivers through local Alzheimer's support groups, add more languages, and connect to doctors' records systems with consent.
  • Later features: scam call screening, payment protection, a wandering alert using phone location, and camera input on a home hub.
  • Beyond dementia: stroke recovery, brain injury, and older adults living alone.
